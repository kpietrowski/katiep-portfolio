Grouped by the version each person first installed, which is near enough the paywall they saw. Written once and never overwritten, so updating doesn’t move anyone between rows. Someone who lingers for weeks and buys after updating stays under the version they started on — intended, not a defect. Buy % is of those who reached the paywall; Reach % is how many got that far at all. “Before tracking” is everyone who installed prior to this being recorded — their first version is unrecoverable, so they sit together and can’t be compared. New rows accumulate from now on.

Tiny Voices upsell

Unlocks are recorded when the grant succeeds, so this counts people who actually got Tiny Voices Pro — not merely people who paid. If it ever trails the subscription sales in RevenueCat, someone paid and wasn't unlocked, and that gap is the thing to chase. The rate's denominator is an estimate: everyone who buys Tide on 1.1.0+ is shown the offer, but the app reports no view event, so it's "could have seen it", not "did see it". People counts individuals; events includes the monthly re-grants that keep an entitlement alive.

Campaigns

CampaignAudienceSent After sendEverUndatedShareLast sent
This is not attribution. “Ever” counts recipients who have since purchased — by any route. Taps aren’t recorded anywhere, so someone who ignored the notification and bought days later from an ad counts the same as someone who tapped it and bought immediately. Since most installs receive a lifecycle campaign eventually, this number drifts toward your total sales rather than a push-driven subset. Read it as a ceiling on what push could be worth, never as its contribution. Rows sent to people who hadn’t yet bought at least guarantee the purchase came after the send — marked after the send. For an audience of everyone, recipients may already have been customers, so even that doesn’t hold and the share is greyed out. Devices that uninstall are removed when Apple reports the token dead, so a long-running campaign undercounts rather than over.
