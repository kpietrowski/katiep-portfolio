The iPhone Posing Guide for Families

I've spent 15 years photographing families, weddings and elopements on Cape Cod. These are my go-to poses: simple, safe for little ones, and natural, not stiff.

How to use this guide: pick two or three poses before you start, not twelve. Babies and toddlers give you about ten good minutes. Plan a few shots, get them, then put the phone down and enjoy your people.

Before you pose: 3 quick rules

  1. Wipe your lens. Your phone lens is smudged right now, I promise. A quick wipe is the difference between hazy and crisp.
  2. Go to the window. Pick the brightest room, open the blinds, and turn off every overhead light and lamp. Soft window light is the secret to every photo I've ever loved.
  3. Clear the corner. A neatly made bed or a plain wall, with the clutter moved out of the shot.

Newborn poses

Shoot right after a feed, when baby is full, warm and sleepy. Keep the room cozy and always have a grown-up within arm's reach. Every pose keeps baby flat, supported and safe.

Pose 01

The swaddled sleeper

Wrap baby snugly in a soft, neutral swaddle and lay them on their back in the middle of a neatly made bed. Turn them so the top of their head points toward the window. Stand over them and shoot straight down.

Katie's tipHold your phone with both hands and keep it to the side of baby, never directly over their face. Kneel on the bed beside baby rather than standing on anything to get higher.
Pose 02

The tiny details

The little things change fastest, so get them now: eyelashes, a curled hand, wrinkly toes, the curve of a tiny ear. Switch to 2x, get close, and tap to focus right on the detail.

Katie's tipHave mom or dad hold baby's foot or hand in theirs. The size difference tells the whole story.
Pose 03

Swaddle off, blanket on

Lay baby on their back on a soft blanket in just a diaper, with a tiny knit or muslin wrap draped loosely over their tummy. Let their arms and legs rest however they naturally fall.

Katie's tipWarm the room up first. A cold baby is a wiggly, unhappy baby.
Pose 04

Sleepy in the bassinet or crib

Photograph baby exactly where they sleep. Stand at the side of the bassinet and shoot down at an angle, so you get their face and a little of the crib around them.

Katie's tipClear out the extras so it's just baby and the sheet. It's simpler, safer and looks so peaceful.

Parent & baby poses

These are the photos your kids will treasure most, so hand the phone off and get in them. Have your partner, a grandparent or a friend shoot, or prop your phone up and use the self-timer.

Pose 05

The window cradle

Stand right next to the window, holding baby in the crook of your arm. Turn your body slightly toward the light and look down at baby. Your photographer stands a few steps back, with the window off to the side.

Katie's tipDon't look at the camera. Look at your baby like no one else is in the room.
Pose 06

Chest snuggle

Lie back on a neatly made bed, propped on pillows, with baby resting tummy-down on your chest and their cheek turned to the side. One hand on their back, the other on their bottom. Shoot from above and slightly to the side.

Katie's tipSkin to skin is magic here. Bare shoulders and a sleepy baby on a plain white sheet are timeless.
Pose 07

The forehead kiss

Hold baby up near your face and close your eyes as you press a slow kiss to their forehead. Your photographer gets close at 2x and focuses on baby's face.

Katie's tipMake it slow. Hold the kiss for a few seconds so your photographer has time to catch it.
Pose 08

Dad's hands

Lay baby along dad's forearm, or cradled in both of his hands, against his chest. Get close and let the frame fill with hands and baby.

Katie's tipShoot this one on a dark t-shirt, and the hands and the baby really pop.

Sibling poses

Big brothers and sisters can be unpredictable, and that's half the charm. Keep it short, keep it fun, and never push a kid who's done. A grown-up's hands should always be right there, just out of frame.

Pose 09

The first hold

Sit big sibling in the middle of the bed with their back against pillows. Lay baby across their lap and arms, with an adult kneeling just out of the shot, hands ready. Shoot from slightly above.

Katie's tipAsk them, "Can you show me how gentle you can be?" You'll get the sweetest focus on their face.
Pose 10

Side by side

Lay baby on their back on the bed, and have big sibling lie down right beside them, snuggled in close with an arm around baby. Stand over the bed and shoot straight down. (Big kid on their tummy, propped on their elbows looking at baby, works beautifully too.)

Katie's tipWhisper a secret to the big kid: "Go tell the baby what your favorite animal is."
Pose 11

A kiss on the head

With baby held securely by a seated parent, have big sibling lean in and kiss the top of baby's head. Get close and shoot at 2x.

Katie's tipShoot several frames in a row. The moment right after the kiss, when they giggle or look up, is often the best one.
Pose 12

Tiny hand, little hand

Have big sibling hold baby's hand or rest their hand next to baby's. Get in close for a detail shot of the two hands together.

Katie's tipThis is the perfect pose for a toddler who won't sit still for anything else.

Whole-family poses

Prop your phone on a shelf, a stack of books or a small tripod at about chest height, set the 10-second self-timer, and jump in. Use 1x for these, never 0.5x.

Pose 13

Everyone on the bed

Parents sit against the headboard with baby in someone's arms and the kids tucked in close on either side. Snuggle in tight with no gaps between bodies.

Katie's tipHave everyone look at the baby instead of the camera. Instant connection, no forced smiles.
Pose 14

The window hug

Stand together by the window, with parents in the middle holding baby and older kids wrapped around legs and waists. Turn everyone slightly toward the light.

Katie's tipTell the kids to "squeeze mom as tight as you can!" You'll get real laughter every time.
Pose 15

The pile-on

Lie down on the bed in a cozy pile, heads close together and baby snuggled safely on a parent's chest. This one needs a helper: have a grandparent or friend stand beside the bed and shoot straight down.

Katie's tipHave the big kids whisper something silly into a parent's ear on the count of three, and keep shooting through the giggles.
Pose 16

Walking in

Have the family walk slowly toward the camera holding hands, through a doorway or down a bright hallway. Shoot several frames in a row as they come.

Katie's tipTell them to look at each other, not at you. Movement makes it feel like a real memory, not a posed portrait.

Prompts for real smiles

"Say cheese" gets you fake smiles. These get you the real ones.

  • "Whisper your favorite food in mom's ear." Instant giggles.
  • "Squeeze each other as tight as you can!" Real hugs, real laughs.
  • "Look at the baby. Now look at the baby's toes." Soft, connected faces.
  • "Can you show me how gentle you can be?" The sweetest sibling moments.
  • "Don't laugh. Whatever you do, don't laugh." Works on every kid, every time.
  • For parents: "Look at each other like you just got home from the hospital." Tears optional.
