The iPhone Posing Guide for Families

I've spent 15 years photographing families, weddings and elopements on Cape Cod. These are my go-to poses: simple, safe for little ones, and natural, not stiff.

How to use this guide: pick two or three poses before you start, not twelve. Babies and toddlers give you about ten good minutes. Plan a few shots, get them, then put the phone down and enjoy your people.

Before you pose: 3 quick rules

  1. Wipe your lens. Your phone lens is smudged right now, I promise. A quick wipe is the difference between hazy and crisp.
  2. Go to the window. Pick the brightest room, open the blinds, and turn off every overhead light and lamp. Soft window light is the secret to every photo I've ever loved.
  3. Clear the corner. A neatly made bed or a plain wall, with the clutter moved out of the shot.

Newborn poses

Shoot right after a feed, when baby is full, warm and sleepy. Keep the room cozy and always have a grown-up within arm's reach. Every pose keeps baby flat, supported and safe.

Pose 01

The swaddled sleeper

Wrap baby snugly in a soft, neutral swaddle and lay them on their back in the middle of a neatly made bed. Turn them so the top of their head points toward the window. Stand over them and shoot straight down.

Katie's tipHold your phone with both hands and keep it to the side of baby, never directly over their face. Kneel on the bed beside baby rather than standing on anything to get higher.
Pose 02

The tiny details

The little things change fastest, so get them now: eyelashes, a curled hand, wrinkly toes, the curve of a tiny ear. Switch to 2x, get close, and tap to focus right on the detail.

Katie's tipHave mom or dad hold baby's foot or hand in theirs. The size difference tells the whole story.
Pose 03

Swaddle off, blanket on

Lay baby on their back on a soft blanket in just a diaper, with a tiny knit or muslin wrap draped loosely over their tummy. Let their arms and legs rest however they naturally fall.

Katie's tipWarm the room up first. A cold baby is a wiggly, unhappy baby.
Pose 04

Awake in the crib

Photograph baby in their crib or bassinet while they're awake and content, with you right there. Stand at the side and shoot down at an angle over the rail, so you get their whole body and a little of the crib around them. The rails framing the edge of the photo make it feel like home.

Katie's tipA favorite stuffed animal in the corner is sweet for the photo, but take it and any pillows out as soon as you're done. For sleep, it's just baby and a fitted sheet.

Parent & baby poses

These are the photos your kids will treasure most, so hand the phone off and get in them. Have your partner, a grandparent or a friend shoot, or prop your phone up and use the self-timer.

Pose 05

The window snuggle

Stand right next to the window and hold baby upright on your shoulder, their cheek resting on you. Turn so the light falls across baby's face, then rest your cheek against their head and look down. Your photographer stands just behind your shoulder and focuses on baby's face.

Katie's tipDon't look at the camera. Look at your baby like no one else is in the room.
Pose 06

Chest snuggle

Lie back on a neatly made bed, propped on pillows, with baby resting tummy-down on your chest and their cheek turned to the side. One hand on their back, the other on their bottom. Shoot from above and slightly to the side.

Katie's tipSkin to skin is magic here. Bare shoulders and a sleepy baby on a plain white sheet are timeless.
Pose 07

The forehead kiss

Lay baby on their back on the bed and lie down beside them. Lean in and press a slow kiss to their forehead with your eyes closed. Your photographer gets in really close, so it's just your two faces filling the frame, and focuses on baby.

Katie's tipMake it slow. Hold the kiss for a few seconds so your photographer has time to catch it.
Pose 08

Dad's hands

Lay baby along dad's forearm, or cradled in both of his hands, against his chest. Get close and let the frame fill with hands and baby.

Katie's tipShoot this one on a dark t-shirt, and the hands and the baby really pop.

Sibling poses

Big brothers and sisters can be unpredictable, and that's half the charm. Keep it short, keep it fun, and never push a kid who's done. A grown-up's hands should always be right there, just out of frame.

Pose 09

The sibling pillow

Have big sibling lie flat on their tummy in the middle of the bed, cheek on the sheet, facing you. A grown-up gently lays baby tummy-down across their back, then keeps both hands right there, an inch from baby, the entire time. Get down low at the edge of the bed, level with their faces, and shoot straight across.

Katie's tipTell big sibling they're baby's bed, so they have to be as still as a statue. Take a few quick frames, then lift baby off. Thirty seconds is plenty, and it's the one they'll ask to see again and again.
Pose 10

Side by side

Lay baby on their back on the bed, and have big sibling lie down right beside them, snuggled in close with an arm around baby. Stand over the bed and shoot straight down. (Big kid on their tummy, propped on their elbows looking at baby, works beautifully too.)

Katie's tipWhisper a secret to the big kid: "Go tell the baby what your favorite animal is."
Pose 11

A kiss on the head

Lay a sleepy baby on their back on the bed and have big sibling lie down beside them, heads close together. Ask them to close their eyes and give baby a soft kiss on the forehead. Stand over the bed and shoot straight down, getting close at 2x. Keep a grown-up's hand right there, just out of frame.

Katie's tipAsk them to hold the kiss for three slow seconds and keep shooting. Eyes closed makes it dreamy, and the moment right after, when they giggle or open their eyes, is often a keeper too.
Pose 12

Tiny hand, little hand

Sit big sibling against pillows or the back of the couch and lay baby in their lap, with an arm wrapped around baby and a grown-up kneeling right beside them. Ask them to hold baby's tiny hand. Stand over them and shoot down, then step in close at 2x for a detail of just the two hands together.

Katie's tipAsk, "Can you give baby a kiss on the head?" while they're holding hands. And if your toddler won't sit still for anything else, the hands-only close-up still works.

Whole-family poses

Prop your phone on a shelf, a stack of books or a small tripod at about chest height, set the 10-second self-timer, and jump in. Use 1x for these, never 0.5x.

Pose 13

Everyone on the bed

Sit side by side on the edge of a neatly made bed, close enough that your shoulders touch. Both parents cradle baby together between you, and let the big kid climb up behind and hang over a shoulder. Set the camera at seated chest height, a few steps back, so you get a little of the room too.

Katie's tipHave the grown-ups look down at the baby and let the toddler do whatever they want. Their curious little face is the best part of the photo.
Pose 14

The lean-in

Stand in front of a plain wall near a window. One parent holds baby along their forearm, with baby's head in their hand. The other stands close at their side, rests their head on the first parent's shoulder and lays a hand on baby. Both of you look down at baby.

Katie's tipLean in and stay there. Let your heads touch and your shoulders relax. Hold the hug for a few breaths and it stops looking posed.
Pose 15

Shoulder ride

Big kid goes up on a parent's shoulders and the other parent holds baby close beside them. Stand against a plain wall with a little space overhead so no one gets cropped. The grown-ups look up at the big kid instead of at the camera.

Katie's tipAsk the big kid to give the top of dad's head a big kiss or to mess up his hair. Real laughs every time.
Pose 16

Big sister's lap

Sit on the bed with the big kid in your lap, and hold baby in front of you both, supported by your arms. Everyone looks at baby. Put the window beside you so the light falls across all three faces. You can shoot this one on the self-timer, or have the other parent take it.

Katie's tipQuietly ask the big kid to count baby's fingers or check if baby is awake. You'll get that focused, tender look.

Prompts for real smiles

"Say cheese" gets you fake smiles. These get you the real ones.

  • "Whisper your favorite food in mom's ear." Instant giggles.
  • "Squeeze each other as tight as you can!" Real hugs, real laughs.
  • "Look at the baby. Now look at the baby's toes." Soft, connected faces.
  • "Can you show me how gentle you can be?" The sweetest sibling moments.
  • "Don't laugh. Whatever you do, don't laugh." Works on every kid, every time.
  • For parents: "Look at each other like you just got home from the hospital." Tears optional.
